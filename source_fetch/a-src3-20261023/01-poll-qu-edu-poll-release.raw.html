      October 21, 2026

      NY Governor's Race: Hochul 55%, Blakeman 41%, Quinnipiac University New York State Poll Finds; Nearly A Quarter Of Voters Still Unfamiliar With Blakeman

      Share

      • mail_outline
      Page Contents

      Page Contents

      • Background

      In the final stretch of New York's gubernatorial race, Democratic incumbent Kathy Hochul receives 55 percent support among likely voters and Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman receives 41 percent support, with 4 percent not choosing a candidate, according to a Quinnipiac (KWIN-uh-pea-ack) University poll of likely voters in New York State released today.

      This compares to Quinnipiac University's September 23 poll, when Hochul received 58 percent support and Blakeman received 39 percent support, with 3 percent not choosing a candidate.

      In today's poll, Democrats 94 - 5 percent back Hochul and Republicans 95 - 4 percent back Blakeman. Among independents, 51 percent back Blakeman and 44 percent back Hochul.

      Women 60 - 35 percent back Hochul. Men are split, with 49 percent backing Hochul and 48 percent backing Blakeman.

      FAVORABILITY RATINGS

      Forty-five percent of likely voters have a favorable opinion of Kathy Hochul, while 46 percent have an unfavorable opinion of her and 6 percent haven't heard enough about her.

      In September, 46 percent of likely voters had a favorable opinion of Hochul, 43 percent had an unfavorable opinion of her, and 10 percent hadn't heard enough about her.

      In today's poll, 36 percent of likely voters have a favorable opinion of Bruce Blakeman, while 40 percent have an unfavorable opinion of him and 23 percent haven't heard enough about him.

      In September, 30 percent of likely voters had a favorable opinion of Blakeman, 36 percent had an unfavorable opinion of him, and 34 percent hadn't heard enough about him.

      "As Republican Bruce Blakeman becomes better known, he's improved his image among independents. But with less than two weeks to go until the final votes are cast, he faces an uphill battle as Hochul maintains a double-digit lead," said Quinnipiac University Poll Assistant Director Mary Snow.

      MOST IMPORTANT ISSUE

      When likely voters were given a list of ten issues and asked which is the most important one in deciding who to vote for in the election for governor, the cost of living (22 percent) and the economy (19 percent) top the list, followed by immigration (13 percent). No other issue reaches double digits.

      Among likely voters who say the cost of living is the most important issue, Hochul leads Blakeman 71 - 24 percent.

      Among likely voters who say the economy is the most important issue, Hochul leads Blakeman 72 - 27 percent.

      Among likely voters who say immigration is the most important issue, Blakeman leads Hochul 71 - 28 percent.

      "The issues driving likely voters to choose a candidate produce starkly different outcomes. If it's the cost of living or the economy as the top issue, Hochul has resounding support. If it's immigration, Blakeman leads overwhelmingly," added Snow.

      HOCHUL JOB APPROVAL

      Forty-nine percent of likely voters approve of the way Kathy Hochul is handling her job as governor, while 45 percent disapprove.

      PRESIDENT TRUMP

      Thirty-four percent of likely voters approve of the way Donald Trump is handling his job as president, while 60 percent disapprove.

      Thirty-five percent of likely voters have a favorable opinion of Donald Trump, 59 percent have an unfavorable opinion of him, and 2 percent haven't heard enough about him.

      ZOHRAN MAMDANI

      Forty-three percent of likely voters have a favorable opinion of Zohran Mamdani, 42 percent have an unfavorable opinion of him, and 12 percent haven't heard enough about him.

      1,167 New York State likely voters were surveyed from October 15th - 19th with a margin of error of +/- 3.8 percentage points, including the design effect.

      The Quinnipiac University Poll, directed by Doug Schwartz, Ph.D. since 1994, conducts independent, non-partisan national and state polls on politics and issues. Surveys adhere to industry best practices and are based on probability-based samples using random digit dialing with live interviewers calling landlines and cell phones.
