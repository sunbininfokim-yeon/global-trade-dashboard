Montana Senate race: Kurt Alme tops Bodnar and Bankhead in new poll findings

HELENA, Mont. - A new poll commissioned through NonStop Local shows Republican candidate Kurt Alme with a sizable lead for the race for the US Senate seat being vacated by Senator Steve Daines.
 
NonStop Local partnered with American Pulse Research and Polling to speak directly with Montanans ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. Here’s where things stand in the race for U.S. Senate: 
 
Kurt Alme, the Republican nominee, leads the field by 19 points. Around 45.1% of Montanans—surveyed via live phone calls and text conversations between September 19 and 24—expressed plans to vote for Alme. Support for Independent Seth Bodnar and Democrat Alani Bankhead just barely trailed behind Alme at 45% combined, with 26.1% of Montanans voicing their support for Bodnar to Bankhead’s 18.9%. 
 
However, the poll found that even if Bankhead were to drop out of the race, Bodnar would still not have enough votes to top Alme. 
 
Among Democratic voters in Montana, 55.9% supported Bankhead for Senate compared to 37.3% for Bodnar. However, the survey found Bodnar draws in more self-identified Independent and Republican voters than Bankhead. 44.8% of Bodnar’s supporters identify as Independents or not affiliated with any party—the largest share—while 38.1% identify as Democrats and 14.5% as Republicans. 
 
The split across candidates may be further colored by how voters view the current state of the Treasure State in 2026, with no clear consensus emerging. 43% of respondents said they feel Montana is generally headed in the right direction, while 38.7% see it heading in the wrong direction, and 17.8% report feeling unsure. Among Montana cities surveyed, Helena reported the highest confidence with 51.4% of responding residents stating they feel Montana is moving in the right direction, and the city with the least confidence was Missoula with 45% of responding residents believing the state is moving in the wrong direction. 
 
Overall, 67.7% of Republican voters said they feel the state is moving in the right direction, with just 13.5% responding the opposite. Just 14.3% of Montana Democrats said the state is moving in the right direction, while 71.2% say it’s moving in the wrong direction. A majority of Independents—around 54.1%—say the state is heading the wrong direction. An equal share of Republicans and Independents reported uncertainty on this question at 19%, while just 14.6% of Democrats reported uncertainty. 
 
American Pulse Research assessed that Bankhead, as the Democratic candidate, may be drawing in some self-identified Democratic voters by default. “Many of [Bankhead’s] supporters have yet to form a substantive impression of her, while Bodnar’s supporters are more positive and more intense in their views of him,” the poll said. 
 
When asked who they would vote for if the election was held today, Alme had the strongest support from voters over the age of 44. 45.5% of voters aged 45-64 said they would vote for Alme, as did 48.6% of voters over the age of 65. 
 
30.9% of voters over the age of 65 said they would vote for Bodnar, while just 13.5% of them said they’d vote for Bankhead. 
 
71.2% of self-described Alme voters said that the U.S. trade dispute with Canada is warranted, compared to just 10.3% and 5.3% of Bodnar and Bankhead voters, respectively. Bodnar and Bankhead voters described similar levels of opinion on whether tariffs have a negative impact. 83.5% of Bodnar voters said tariffs have a negative impact, while 88.4% of Bankhead voters said the same. Around 27.4% of Alme voters expressed tariffs having a negative impact. 
 
Across the entire Treasure State, 80.4% of all voters say a candidate’s stance on data centers is important to them, with 50.3% of that group calling it “very important.” 
 
“The Senate ballot shows a substantial gap between Alme and each individual opponent, while the Bodnar-Bankhead rivalry involves distinct—and partly overlapping—coalitions,” the poll’s concluding findings states. 
 
Over 600 Montanans were surveyed for how they plan to vote in the November elections. The survey was taken September 19 through September 24.
