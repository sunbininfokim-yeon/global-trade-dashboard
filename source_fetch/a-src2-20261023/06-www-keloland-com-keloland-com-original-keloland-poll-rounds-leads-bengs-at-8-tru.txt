KELOLAND Poll: Rounds leads Bengs at 8%, Trump approval down
Rae Yost
9 hours ago

SIOUX FALLS, S.D. (KELO) — Eight points separate Independent candidate Brian Bengs and incumbent Republican Mike Rounds for one of South Dakota’s U.S. Senate seats, according to a new poll from KELOLAND News.

KELOLAND worked with long-time partner Emerson College on a poll that surveyed 803 likely South Dakota voters and has a margin of error of plus or minus 3.5%.




The poll shows 50% of the voters support Rounds while 42% support Bengs. Eight percent of the voters are undecided. The 50% to 42% split is how voters would vote that day and if they had already voted, who they voted for.

The survey was conducted on Oct. 19 and 20 and includes results from the governor’s race, presidential approval rating, constitutional amendments and two issues for voters. Absentee voting is underway in South Dakota and Election Day is set for Nov. 3.

Trump approval rating drops in South Dakota

A drop in President Donald Trump’s approval rating could have some influence on the Rounds vs. Bengs race. Trump’s approval has dropped at least 10 points since the March poll.

In the latest poll, Trump has a 44% job approval rating with 52% saying they disapprove.

A KELOLAND/Emerson Poll in May had Trump’s approval rating at  53% compared to 55% in March.

Trump had endorsed Rounds in 2025, before Rounds officially announced he was running for re-election.

The poll was conducted after absentee voting started. Bengs has the lead among those who have voted with 55% compared to 45% for Rounds.

If the numbers hold at 50% for Rounds and 42% for Bengs, this would be one of Rounds’ closest races since at least his state senate race in 1990 where he won with 53% to 47% of the vote.

Ballot questions

Surveyed voters are split on the four constitutional amendments on the ballot. Most opposed Amendment L at 60%. The amendment would require a proposed constitutional amendment to receive 60% in favor of all votes cast. There were 11% undecided on Amendment L.

Nearly the same percentage 59%, oppose Amendment I. This amendment repeals expanded Medicaid coverage if federal funding for the programs drops below 90%.There were 13% undecided on Amendment I.

The support is at 64% for Amendment J which clarifies that an individual must be a citizen of the U.S. to be eligible to vote. Only 3% of the voters were undecided of support or opposition.

A smaller margin at 41% support Amendment K. This would establish a trust for unclaimed property fund. There were 29% undecided on Amendment K.

Rhoden leads Ahlers

Gov. Larry Rhoden has a commanding lead in the South Dakota governor’s race with 56% of the surveyed voters. Democrat Dan Ahlers has 38% of the vote. That’s for people who are likely to vote or who have already voted.

In 2022, Republican Gov. Kristi Noem was re-elected with 62% of the vote to 35% for Democrat Jamie Smith.

Data centers, death penalty

Most voters do not want a hyperscale data center to be built in the state. The poll shows 57% are opposed to hyperscale data centers being built in South Dakota. Thirty-five percent are not opposed while 8% are neutral.

Hyperscale data centers are getting significant attention in the state as local governments discuss possible moratoriums or guidelines and organized groups have shared opposition or shared concerns about them.

A bill to repeal the death penalty was introduced in the state House this year. House Bill 1268 was rejected in the legislature. The state of Tennessee also failed in two attempts to lethally inject an inmate this year.

The KELOLAND/Emerson Poll asked voters about support for the death penalty. Sixty-three percent of voters support the death penalty and 28% are opposed with 9% as neutral.
