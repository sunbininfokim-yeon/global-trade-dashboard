CA-11 Poll: Wiener Holds Slight Lead over Chan in San Francisco’s Open House Seat

Date: October 22, 2026

Fielded
Oct. 16–19, 2026
Sample
454 likely voters
Margin of error
±4.6 points
Languages
English, Spanish, Chinese/Mandarin/Cantonese, Korean, Vietnamese
FULL RELEASE & RESULTS →

U.S. House: CA-11

Scott Wiener holds a slight lead over Connie Chan in the open seat election for U.S. House in California’s 11th Congressional District. Among likely voters, Scott Wiener holds a 47% to 41% lead over Connie Chan with 9% undecided and 3% not planning to vote in this U.S. House contest. When the undecided voters are pushed to make a selection, Wiener leads 51% to 44% over Chan with 5% not planning to vote in this U.S. House contest. Both of these leads are just within the margin of error, suggesting a competitive race just two weeks from Election Day.

Approval Ratings

Mayor Daniel Lurie has sky-high approval among San Francisco voters in the 11th congressional district with a net +37 approval. Current Representative Nancy Pelosi follows at a net +23 approval, while Governor Gavin Newsom closely follows at a net +21 approval.

Capitalism, Socialism, and the Democratic Party

Voters in CA-11 also have a Jekyll and Hyde set of opinions on capitalism and socialism. In tech-driven yet progressive San Francisco, a large plurality of voters rate capitalism positively and a large plurality of voters also approve of socialism. The battle for the soul of the Democratic party appears to have divided voters in San Francisco, with equal numbers approving and disapproving of the Democratic Party and Democratic Socialists of America.

Favorite Son, 2028?

San Francisco Democratic voters in CA-11 choose Gavin Newsom over Kamala Harris when asked who they would vote for in a head-to-head 2028 Democratic presidential primary. We asked Democratic voters to choose only between Newsom and Harris for 2028 president. While both Newsom and Harris have deep ties to the Bay Area, 58% of San Francisco Democratic voters in CA-11 would choose Newsom over Harris in a head-to-head 2028 primary and 29% would choose Harris over Newsom. The remainder of Democratic voters (13%) said they would not participate in this election if these were the only two choices. Under an open-ended response, other potential candidates are more popular, with 21% of respondents naming Pete Buttigieg, 20% for Jon Ossoff, 18% for Gavin Newsom, and 10% for Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez over 5% for Kamala Harris.

Survey Methodology

This survey of 454 California voters from October 16-19 is representative of the California likely voter electorate in CA-11. The overall poll has a margin of error of +/- 4.6% points; with larger margins of error for subgroups and crosstabs. Voters were randomly sampled directly from the California voter file, ensuring equal probability of participation for all voters; and to provide representativeness of the CA-11’s voters. Then, voters were screened for those who said they were "very likely" or "somewhat likely" to vote; or who stated they had already voted. The poll was conducted via email to the probability-based sample of voters directly and randomly sampled off the California voter file. Survey weights standard in the field were used to adjust the full sample, so the final results are representative of the likely California CA-11 electorate. The survey weights included demographic variables such as party, race/ethnicity, gender, and age. A raking procedure using the anesrake method in the statistical program R was conducted. The survey was offered in English, and respondents also had the option to take the survey in Spanish, Chinese/Mandarin/Cantonese, Korean, and Vietnamese.
